# Overview

Before learning how to move around the badminton court, you first need to understand **where you're moving.**

Many beginners think of the badminton court as one large rectangle.

Experienced players don't.

Instead, they mentally divide the court into different zones and constantly adjust their position depending on:

- where the shuttle is,
- where the opponent is,
- and what shot they expect next.

Understanding these court zones makes every future lesson—from footwork to tactics—much easier to understand.

# Why Court Awareness Matters

Good badminton isn't about reacting after the shuttle has been hit.

It's about already knowing:

- where you are,
- where your opponent is,
- where the shuttle is likely to go next.

The better you understand the court, the easier movement becomes.

Think of it like driving.

You don't think about turning the steering wheel.

You understand the road.

Court awareness works the same way.

---

# The Three Main Court Zones

Although the badminton court looks simple, it can be divided into three main areas.

## Front Court

The area closest to the net.

Typical shots:

- Net shot
- Net kill
- Net lift
- Short serve

Movement usually finishes with a controlled lunge.

---

## Mid Court

The area around the service line.

Typical shots:

- Drives
- Pushes
- Flat exchanges
- Defensive blocks

Movement here is generally short and explosive.

---

## Rear Court

The area behind the doubles service line.

Typical shots:

- Clears
- Smashes
- Drop shots

Rear-court movement usually requires larger footwork patterns and good recovery.

---

# Forehand Side & Backhand Side

The court is also divided by your body.

## Forehand Side

The side where you naturally hit forehand shots.

---

## Backhand Side

The opposite side.

As you improve, you'll learn techniques that allow you to cover much of this area using around-the-head forehand shots.

For now, simply understand the distinction.

---

# The Six Corners

Most coaches simplify movement by dividing the court into six corners.

```text
Rear Left            Rear Right

Mid Left              Mid Right

Front Left          Front Right
```

Every rally is simply movement between these six areas.

The purpose of footwork is not to memorise steps.

The purpose is to reach each corner efficiently and recover again.

---

## Principle #5

> **Understand the destination before choosing the movement.**

Don't think:

"I need a chasse step."

Think:

"The shuttle is travelling to my forehand front corner."

Once you know **where** you're going, choosing **how** to get there becomes much easier.

---

# What is Base Position?

One of the biggest misconceptions in badminton is that players should always recover to the centre of the court.

That isn't true.

Instead, players recover to their **base position**.

Your base position is the location that gives you the best chance of reaching your opponent's next shot.

Sometimes that is close to the centre.

Sometimes it isn't.

Your base position changes depending on:

- your previous shot,
- your opponent's position,
- whether you're attacking or defending,
- whether you're playing singles or doubles.

You'll explore this concept in much more detail later in the academy.

For now, simply remember:

> **Base position is dynamic.**

# Solo Practice

## Drill 1 — Walk the Court

Without a racket:

Walk slowly to each of the six corners.

Return to your base.

Repeat several times.

Focus on recognising each court zone.

---

## Drill 2 — Call the Corner

Stand in your base position.

Call out a random corner.

Move there.

Return.

Repeat.

---

## Drill 3 — Visualisation

Stand in your ready position.

Imagine:

- Front Left
- Rear Right
- Mid Left
- Front Right

Visualise where you would move before actually moving.

This develops court awareness.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Thinking of the court as one large rectangle.
- Standing in the exact centre after every shot.
- Looking only at the shuttle.
- Forgetting where your base position should be.
- Moving before deciding where you're going.

# Key Takeaways

- Divide the court into zones instead of seeing one large area.

- Base position changes during a rally.
- Good movement starts with understanding the court.
- Efficient footwork begins with good court awareness.
