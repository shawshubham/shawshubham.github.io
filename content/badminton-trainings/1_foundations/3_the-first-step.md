## 1. Overview

---

Many beginners believe fast footwork comes from moving their legs quickly.

In reality, great footwork starts with **one correct first step**.

Professional players don't waste movement. They don't take unnecessary steps or run around the court.

Instead, they generate explosive movement from the ground and let one powerful first step take them towards the shuttle.

This lesson teaches the foundation of every movement you'll make on a badminton court.

## 3. Why The First Step Matters

---

Every movement in badminton follows the same sequence.

```text
     Recover
        ↓
  Ready Position
        ↓
   Read Opponent
        ↓
    Split Step
        ↓
    First Step
        ↓
 Movement Pattern
        ↓
    Play Shot
        ↓
     Recover
```

Notice something important.

The first step comes **before** every footwork pattern.

Whether you later perform:

- a chasse step,
- a crossover,
- a lunge,
- or a scissor kick,

everything starts with one explosive movement.

A poor first step means every movement afterwards becomes slower and less efficient.

---

## 4. Principle #4

---

> **Push the court away. Don't reach for the shuttle.**

One of the biggest mistakes beginners make is reaching with their leading foot.

Instead of pulling themselves towards the shuttle, they should push powerfully away from the court using the opposite leg.

Think of the ground as your source of power.

The stronger your push, the easier movement becomes.

---

## 5. Push vs Reach

---

Imagine the shuttle lands to your forehand side.

Many beginners do this:

```text
Reach

🧍──────►
```

Their body stretches towards the shuttle.

Balance is lost.

Recovery becomes difficult.

Instead, good players do this:

```text
Push

⬅️ Force

🧍►
```

They push explosively from the opposite leg.

The body naturally accelerates towards the shuttle.

This movement is:

- faster,
- more balanced,
- and easier to recover from.

---

## 6. Explosive Push-Off

---

Your first step should feel like pushing yourself away from the floor.

Think about a sprinter leaving the starting blocks.

They don't pull themselves forward.

They drive against the ground.

Badminton works the same way.

A powerful first step comes from:

- bent knees,
- loaded legs,
- weight on the balls of the feet,
- explosive push-off.

Not from taking lots of tiny steps.

## 8. Moving in Different Directions

---

Although every movement starts with the same principles, the first step changes depending on where the shuttle is travelling.

### Forward

Push from the rear leg.

Stay low.

Move towards the shuttle without standing upright.

---

### Backward

Rotate your body.

Push explosively.

Avoid running backwards.

---

### Sideways

Push from the opposite leg.

Stay balanced.

Avoid crossing your feet unnecessarily.

---

**Remember:**

Different directions.

Same principles.

## 9. Solo Drills

---

### Drill 1 – Push & Stop

```
Split Step
    ↓
   Push
    ↓
 One Step
    ↓
   Stop
    ↓
 Recover
```

Repeat:

- Forward × 20
- Backward × 20
- Left × 20
- Right × 20

Focus on quality, not speed.

---

### Drill 2 – First Step Reaction

Imagine an opponent.

After every split step, randomly choose a direction.

Explode with one clean first step.

Recover.

Repeat for 2–3 minutes.

---

### Drill 3 – Mirror Practice

Stand in front of a mirror.

Watch:

- knee bend,
- balance,
- body lean,
- first step,
- recovery.

Correct technique before increasing speed.

---

## 10. Partner Drill

---

Ask your partner to point randomly to different corners.

Your sequence should always be:

```text
Ready Position
     ↓
  Split Step
     ↓
 First Step
     ↓
   Stop
     ↓
  Recover
```

Do not worry about reaching the shuttle.

Focus only on producing a powerful and balanced first step.

## 12. Common Mistakes

---

Avoid:

- reaching instead of pushing,
- standing upright,
- taking many small steps,
- pushing from the wrong leg,
- looking down,
- forgetting to recover,
- trying to move fast before moving correctly.

## 13. Key Takeaways

---

- Every movement starts with the first step.
- Push from the ground instead of reaching.
- Stay low and balanced.
- Build speed through efficiency, not effort.
- Good footwork begins long before the second step.
