## 1. Overview

---

Everything in badminton starts with the grip.

Whether you are playing a clear, smash, drop shot, net shot, drive, lift, or serve, your grip controls:

- power
- accuracy
- racket angle
- deception
- finger control
- injury prevention

Many recreational players struggle with advanced shots not because they are weak, but because they hold the racket incorrectly.

This lesson should be revisited regularly, even after you move to intermediate skills.

## 5. 4 Major Types of Grip

---

### 5.1 Forehand Grip

The forehand grip is the first grip you should learn.

### Used For

- forehand clear
- smash
- drop shot
- forehand lift
- forehand drive
- high serve

### How To Hold It

Imagine shaking someone's hand.

Now place the racket in that same position.

Your thumb and index finger should form a relaxed V shape on the handle.

The racket should feel secure, but your hand should not feel tense.

> **Coach's Tip**
>
> If your palm is directly behind the racket strings, you are probably using a panhandle grip instead of a forehand grip.

---

## 5.2 Backhand Grip

The backhand grip is used when the shuttle comes to your non-racket side or when you need thumb power.

### Used For

- backhand serve
- backhand net shot
- backhand lift
- backhand drive
- defensive backhand blocks

### How To Hold It

Start from the forehand grip.

Rotate the racket slightly.

Place your thumb against the wider flat bevel of the handle.

Your thumb should support the shot instead of wrapping tightly around the grip.

> **Common Mistake**
>
> Many beginners try to play backhand shots with a forehand grip. This reduces control and makes the shot feel weak.

---

## 5.3 Bevel Grip

The bevel grip sits between the forehand and backhand grip.

### Used For

- around-the-head shots
- some defensive shots
- transitional strokes
- awkward rear-court positions

You do not need to master this immediately, but you should know it exists.

As you improve, the bevel grip becomes useful for shots where the shuttle is not perfectly on your forehand or backhand side.

---

## 5.4 Panhandle Grip

The panhandle grip is when the racket is held more like a frying pan.

### Used For

- some net kills
- some forecourt interceptions
- emergency shots in front of the body

### Warning

This should not be your default grip.

Many beginners naturally use the panhandle grip because it feels easy at first, but it limits:

- clears
- smashes
- drops
- overhead technique

> **Important**
>
> Avoid using the panhandle grip for rear-court overhead shots. It will make your overhead technique harder to develop.

---

## 6. Grip Pressure

---

Do not squeeze the racket tightly all the time.

Use this pattern:

```text
Relaxed grip during preparation
        ↓
Finger squeeze at impact
        ↓
Relax again after the shot
```

This improves:

- racket speed
- power
- recovery
- control
- endurance

> **Key Point**
>
> Power in badminton does **not** come from holding the racket harder.
>
> It comes from the combination of:
>
> - correct timing,
> - proper technique,
> - body rotation,
> - and efficient finger control.
>
> A relaxed grip allows the racket to accelerate more naturally, while tightening your fingers **only at the moment of impact** helps transfer power into the shuttle.

---

## 7. Finger Power

---

One of the biggest differences between badminton and many other racket sports is the use of **finger power**.

While your shoulder, forearm, and wrist all contribute to the stroke, much of the racket acceleration comes from your fingers tightening around the grip at the moment of impact.

This becomes increasingly important for:

- Drives
- Net kills
- Defensive blocks
- Fast interceptions
- Smash power
- Deceptive shots

### How to Practise Finger Power

Hold the racket using a relaxed forehand grip.

Without swinging your arm, practise squeezing the handle using only your fingers.

You should notice the racket head moving slightly with each squeeze.

Repeat:

- 3 sets × 20 repetitions

The movement should feel controlled rather than forceful.

> 💡 **Coach's Tip**
>
> Stay relaxed during preparation. The fingers should tighten only at the moment of impact before relaxing again.

## 8. Changing Grips

---

Badminton rallies happen quickly, so you should not expect to use a single grip throughout an entire rally.

Instead, your grip should change naturally depending on the incoming shuttle and the shot you intend to play.

For example:

```text
Forehand Clear
        ↓
Recover
        ↓
Backhand Lift
        ↓
Forehand Drive
        ↓
Net Kill
```

Each of these shots requires a slightly different grip.

Experienced players change grips instinctively without looking at the racket.

As a beginner, focus on changing grips **slowly and correctly**. Speed will come naturally with practice.

## 9. Solo Practice

---

### Drill 1 — Grip Switching

### Objective

Develop the ability to change grips without looking at the racket.

### Steps

1. Hold the racket in a forehand grip.
2. Rotate to the backhand grip.
3. Return to the forehand grip.
4. Repeat continuously.

### Duration

- 2–3 minutes

### Focus

- Smooth movement
- Relaxed fingers
- No visual assistance

---

### Drill 2 — Shadow Swings

Using the correct grip, perform:

- 20 Forehand Shadow Swings
- 20 Backhand Shadow Swings
- 20 Overhead Shadow Swings

Focus on:

- Correct grip
- Relaxed wrist
- Smooth racket path
- Proper preparation

Don't worry about power.

At this stage, good technique is far more important.

---

### Drill 3 — Finger Squeeze Exercise

Hold the racket using a relaxed forehand grip.

Without moving your arm:

- Squeeze the handle.
- Relax.
- Repeat.

### Repetitions

- 30–50 repetitions

This develops finger control and helps prepare for more advanced strokes later.

## 10. Partner Drill

---

This is an excellent drill for two players.

One player calls out:

```text
Forehand
Backhand
Forehand
Backhand
Panhandle
Forehand
Backhand
```

The second player immediately changes to the correct grip.

Start slowly.

Once you become comfortable, gradually increase the speed.

### Goal

Eventually, you should be able to change grips instantly without needing to look at your racket.

## 12. Common Mistakes

Many beginners develop bad habits early.

Avoid these common mistakes:

- Holding the racket too tightly.
- Using the panhandle grip for every overhead shot.
- Never changing grips during rallies.
- Looking at the racket while changing grips.
- Trying to generate power using only the arm.
- Wrapping the thumb around the handle during backhand shots.
- Rushing into advanced strokes before mastering the basics.

### Key Takeaways

- Grip is the foundation of every badminton stroke.
- Learn the forehand and backhand grips before anything else.
- Stay relaxed during preparation and tighten your fingers only at impact.
- Develop finger power instead of relying solely on arm strength.
- Learn to change grips naturally throughout a rally.
- Good grip habits make every future skill easier to learn.
