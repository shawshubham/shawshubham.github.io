Ready Position, Split Step & Court Preparation

Last updated on: 4 Jul, 2026

Overview

Badminton is often called the fastest racket sport in the world.

At higher levels, players don’t wait until they see the shuttle before they start moving.

Instead, they prepare before the opponent hits the shuttle.

This preparation consists of three simple but extremely important concepts:

  • Ready Position
  • Split Step
  • Recovery

Every movement you make on court starts with these three.

Whether you are playing:

  • a clear
  • a smash
  • a drop shot
  • a net shot
  • a defensive block

you will always repeat the same movement cycle.

The Badminton Movement Cycle

Before learning movement, understand this cycle.

Recover
        ↓
Ready Position
        ↓
Read Opponent
        ↓
Split Step
        ↓
Move
        ↓
Play Shot
        ↓
Recover

Every rally is simply this cycle repeated over and over.

Principle #1

Always Recover.

After every shot, your first job is not to admire your shot.

Your first job is to recover and prepare for the next one.

What is the Ready Position?

The ready position is your neutral position before moving to the shuttle.

Think of it as the athletic position used in almost every sport.

A good ready position allows you to move in any direction quickly.

Correct Ready Position

Your feet should be:

  • slightly wider than shoulder width

Your knees should be:

  • slightly bent

Your body weight should be:

  • on the balls of your feet

Your racket should be:

  • in front of your body
  • slightly above waist height

Your eyes should be:

  • watching your opponent

Common Mistakes

Avoid:

  • standing upright
  • locking your knees
  • placing weight on your heels
  • holding the racket too low
  • watching the shuttle instead of the opponent

What is the Split Step?

The split step is a small hop performed just before your opponent strikes the shuttle.

It is not a jump.

It is a timing movement.

The purpose is to preload your legs like a spring so you can explode in any direction.

Why Does the Split Step Work?

Imagine trying to sprint from a completely still position.

Now imagine taking a tiny hop first.

The second feels much quicker.

That is exactly what happens in badminton.

The split step:

  • stores elastic energy
  • improves reaction speed
  • improves balance
  • reduces recovery time

Principle #2

Move after the opponent hits the shuttle—not before.

If you move too early, your opponent can easily change direction and wrong-foot you.

Solo Drills

Drill 1 — Ready Position

Stand in ready position.

Hold for 30 seconds.

Relax.

Repeat 5 times.

Focus on:

  • staying balanced
  • keeping the racket up
  • remaining relaxed

Drill 2 — Split Step

Perform:

Split Step

↓

Recover

Repeat

30 repetitions.

No shuttle required.

Drill 3 — Split & Move

Split

↓

Move Forward

↓

Recover

Repeat 20 times.

Repeat for:

  • backward
  • left
  • right

Partner Drill

Ask your partner to point randomly to different corners.

Every movement should begin with:

Split Step

↓

Move

↓

Recover

The goal is not speed.

The goal is correct movement.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Standing too upright.
  • Jumping too high during the split step.
  • Moving before splitting.
  • Remaining flat-footed.
  • Forgetting to recover.
  • Watching the shuttle instead of the opponent.

Completion Checklist

Before moving on:

  • I can maintain the ready position comfortably.
  • I understand why the split step exists.
  • I can time my split step correctly.
  • I recover after every movement.
  • I completed today’s practice session.

Key Takeaways

  • Every rally begins with preparation.
  • Stay balanced.
  • Keep your racket up.
  • Split just before your opponent hits.
  • Recover after every shot.
  • Good movement is more important than fast movement.
